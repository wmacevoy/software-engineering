# The pair-programming studies behind the GAPS related-work paragraph

- **Supports:** [GAPS](../GAPS.md#related-work), *Related work* under the agent trials: the 90%
  claim, the 15/15 restatement, and the meta-analysis.
- **Sources:**
  - Williams, Kessler, Cunningham, and Jeffries, "Strengthening the Case for Pair Programming",
    *IEEE Software* 17(4), 2000.
  - Cockburn and Williams, "The Costs and Benefits of Pair Programming", XP2000; in *Extreme
    Programming Examined* (2001).
  - Erdogmus and Williams, "The Economics of Software Development by Pair Programmers", *The
    Engineering Economist* 48(4), 2003.
  - Hannay, Dybå, Arisholm, and Sjøberg, "The effectiveness of pair programming: A meta-analysis",
    *Information and Software Technology* 51(7), 2009, DOI 10.1016/j.infsof.2009.02.001.

- **Stored:** no.
- **Status:** not opened here. What the paragraph rests on, as the delegated check reported: the
  Williams paper reports test-case pass rates (individuals 73–78%, pairs 86–94%) and extra
  programmer-hours that fell from 60% to about 15% after an adjustment period; "15% fewer defects
  for 15% more time" is Cockburn and Williams's restatement; Erdogmus and Williams note the effort
  difference was not statistically significant; Hannay et al. found a small positive effect on
  quality and a medium negative effect on effort; no controlled study supports "90% fewer errors",
  the nearest being a single industrial self-report quoted by Erdogmus and Williams. Owed: the
  passages and their checksums.
